Compiled on the coastal plain between the pier and the third groyne, at dawn, at 34 km/h.

Note 01 — on the flight line

Pelicans do not fly alone. They lock into a V, each bird riding the updraft left by the wingtip ahead of it, trading the lead in turn so that nobody burns out first. Cyclists copied the idea and called it a peloton. The pelicans, generously, called it a compliment — and then added a bicycle, on the theory that if you are going to sit in a line you may as well sit in a comfortable one.

Note 03 — on the descent

Downhill is where the specimen comes alive: wings tucked, pouch streaming behind like a flagon of soup, the casquette somehow still on. It reaches thirty-four kilometres an hour and shouts the entire way, which the gulls have asked it to stop doing. On the flat it struggles; on the climb it negotiates. It has never once been seen walking the bike up a kerb.

Nobody asks the pelican how it learned. They just ask why it is on the bicycle.— Field notebook, page 118
