  1. 01

    Gular hydrodynamics in transit

    The throat pouch is not a pannier, strictly speaking, but it is functionally one. During the commute it stays partially inflated, acting as a live ballast bag: fish shift forward on the uphill, and the bird compensates by leaning into the bar. Note the wobble frequency — it matches the pedal stroke almost exactly, which tells you the animal is comfortable, not merely coping.

    Sighted 04:12 · tide falling
  2. 02

    Cadence, and the tyranny of the single gear

    A fixed cog, no freewheel, no brakes. The pelican pedals at a near-metronomic 42 rpm and has been observed holding the same cadence for two hours without snack. Headwind forces a standup posture; the tail feathers splay as an air brake, which is the closest this species comes to admitting a gear exists.

    Cadence logged · 2 h 04 m
  3. 03

    The wing on the bar

    One wing is permanently draped over the handlebar like a passenger's arm out a car window. The other is folded and tucked. This is an aesthetic choice, and the bird knows it. When startled, both wings deploy instantly, the bicycle does not, and the result is a brief, spectacular, entirely unsteerable glide.
