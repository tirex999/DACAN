01 — Anatomy of the commit

Ninety percent beak.
The other ten is pedalling.

Hover the numbered markers. Each leader line points at the exact structure that makes the whole thing work — from the hydroelastic pouch hinge to the wing-driven crank throw.

Specimen log

Marked: juvenile-adult, 3.6 m. Shoe size: none. Notable: refuses a tricycle, “for dignity”.

At 14 knots the pelican is not flying and not walking. It has entered the third, least-documented of its gaits: a seated, forward-leaning shuffle in which the wings drive a 170 mm crank throw while the feet — absurdly, superbly — do nothing at all.

The beak leads. A bill two-thirds the length of the body shifts the whole centre of mass forward of the bottom bracket, which is exactly why the bicycle has a long, raked fork and no front brake: the animal is the counterweight.

  • DrivoWing-driven crank. Elbow acts as the pin, two-bone IK keeps the joint inside anatomical bounds at every angle.
  • GearzDirect-drive rear hub + a decorative 34T chainring for the benefit of purists.
  • AeroPouch-as-fairing. Pleat count varies with wind speed; gust mode is included in this build.
  • KitHelmet, goggles, scarf. The scarf is non-regulation and has been formally complained about twice.
Conclusion of the boardThe pelican is, in fact, riding the bicycle. The bicycle is not riding the pelican. Case closed.
