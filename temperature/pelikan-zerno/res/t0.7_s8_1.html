Pelecanus velocipedus. A plump, golden, impossibly optimistic commuter — observed at 40 frames per second on the dune road, two hours before the tide.

Five features that make the machine work.

01

Head, beak & the cycling cap

The beak is a pair of long, light keratin levers — 40 cm of them. The cap is purely for morale, and it is the only part of the rig that serves no mechanical purpose whatsoever.

02

The gular pump

The pouch is not a storage bag; it is a scoop. Eleven litres of seawater funnelled out, fish retained, everything swallowed head-first. Today's cargo: one mackerel, still indignant.

03

Wings on the handlebars

The wing grips the bar the way a hand would. Primaries fan out on the down-stroke for balance, and tuck flat against the barrel when the onshore wind kicks up.

04

Webbed, not clawed

No toes for toe-clips. The foot is a paddle, so the pedals are flat planks, kicked through at 84 rpm with the knee leading — a bird's knee, which sits further forward than you'd expect.

05

Scarf, breast & the rudder tail

Seventeen cervical vertebrae act as a suspension coil over the cobbles. The tail feathers fan as a rudder, and the scarf streams at roughly the same speed as the good news.
