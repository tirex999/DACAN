PELICAN ON A bicycle

Drawn from life on the south boardwalk, where the light goes apricot at seven and a five-kilogram bird discovers that legs, given a crank, will turn forever. Note the pouch: it is doing nothing. It is, as always, full of optimism.

FIELD NOTES
on a bird built for none of this

Five observations, filed the same evening, beside a bird that has no business on two wheels.

01
The pouch

The gular pouch holds up to 11 litres of water and fish — roughly three times the volume of the pelican's own stomach. It is a net, not a lunchbox.

02
The landing

A four-kilo bird dives from 15 metres at about 40 km/h. Air sacs beneath the skin compress on impact, acting as built-in bumpers.

03
The crossing

Britain's pelican crossing is a pedestrian signal with a beacon — the bird got the honour, not the bicycle. He is aware of the injustice.

04
The flock

In formation they ride the upwash left by the bird ahead, cutting the energy bill by 20–30%. Cycling, it turns out, is a flock behaviour.

05
The passenger

Wingspan 2.5 m. Leg length relative to crank: inadequate. Salt glands above the eye let him drink the sea he rides past.

Drawn on the south boardwalk — the bell was real.
