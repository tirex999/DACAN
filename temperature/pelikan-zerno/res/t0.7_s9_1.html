Field notes

On the
aerodynamics
of a loaded bird

The pelican does not so much pedal as authorise the bicycle. Weight sits high and slightly behind the saddle, the pouch acts as a suspended ballast tank, and the scarf serves as a crude wind indicator — when it streams flat, the bird is doing twenty-eight knots.

Observed at the dune road, 05:40. The subject mounts from a standstill, rings the bell twice as a courtesy to the gulls, and coasts the whole descent with the feet just clear of the ground. Landing is by beak-first braking: the pouch drops, the drag spikes, the bicycle stops politely.
