01

The shuffling four-beat pedalling gait.

Webbed feet slide the pedals in a flat, scraping arc — the same motion used to wade out of shallows, rotated ninety degrees. Cadence stays low, torque stays high. He never spins past 92 rpm and he has never once needed to.

Recorded 06:41 · tide falling · boardwalk, north run
02

Wings are for the bars.

Both primaries grip the tape; both elbows act as suspension. Steering input comes mostly from the neck, which is seven feet of counterweight and reads as a single very opinionated gyroscope. The helmet is decorative. He insists it is not.

Note — his wingspan exceeds the handlebar width by 1.9 m
03

A pouch that steers the machine.

Twelve litres of elastic throat skin, filled with fish, water and, on Tuesday, one lost sandwich. Ballast shifts forward under braking; the front wheel loads up and the whole bicycle becomes honest about its geometry.

Specimen mass 7.4 kg · cargo allowance 2.1 kg
04

He rides the sea's own motorway.

Riders exploit the updraft off the boardwalk rail exactly as they exploit wave crests — a line of pressure, read with the beak tip, at four centimetres above the deck. At nine knots the pouch inflates and doubles as a spinnaker. He knows this. He does not discuss it.

Drag coefficient, pouch deployed: 0.31 → 0.19
05

Skreee, at the opening whistle.

Startled, he bellows from the trachea — a guttural two-syllable horn, audible over surf, traffic and brass bands. Spectators cheer. The sound costs nothing and wins the pavement every time.

Peak 96 dB @ 1 m · duration 0.4 s
03

Anatomy of the machine & its rider

  • Cranks & chainring42 mm arms · 27T ring · a 7.5 kg bird puts down 61 N per stroke, mostly at the bottom of the stroke where the toes splay.
  • Saddle, raced lowSet 130 mm beneath the hip. Low saddle, high neck. The knees never rise above the bar and the tail clears the seat post by a hand's width.
  • Gular pouch as ballast11–12 litres, split 60/40 fore-aft. Inflated at speed it flattens the drag curve; loaded at rest it plants the front wheel for the boardwalk starts.
  • Drop bar, wing-mountedHands sit on the primaries, not the hooks. Steering leverage is transmitted through the wingspan, so the frame is built stiff and the rider is built loose.
  • Fixed cog, 13TNo brakes. Back-pressure is the brake: 404 mm of wheelbase and a very long tail do the rest of the stopping, theatrically.
  • Ocular tracking, 360°The eye reads the surf line and the traffic at once. Helmet vents: three. Helmet function: entirely social.

05

Field tape — the long crossing

06:52:04 — wind from the water, eleven knots. He walks the bicycle to the rail and stands there, one webbed foot on the deck, absurdly still, for a full eleven seconds.

06:52:19 — mounting: a single hop, wings once, then weight. The frame shudders. A gull abandons the whole argument immediately.

06:53:41 — cruising, beak tip eight centimetres off the boards, reading the pressure in the deck like a printed page. The pouch is full of the morning's anchovy and sways one hand's width ahead of the down tube.

06:55:02 — startled by the ice-cream bell. Two-syllable horn, wings off the bars, 1.4 metres of lateral wobble, then composure so complete it reads as arrogance.

06:57:30 — out of sight past the pier. Sea quiet. Deck marks: two tyre tracks and one webbed drag line, forty-one centimetres long.
