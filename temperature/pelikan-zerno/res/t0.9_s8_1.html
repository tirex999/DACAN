The gular hydraulic net

The pouch is not a bucket — it's a jaw-hinged net that expands in about twenty milliseconds and can carry eleven litres of fish-and-seawater slurry, roughly three times the volume of the bird's own gut. It strains the water out through the beak's serrated edges before the swallow.

02

Bones like window glass

Pelican long bones are so thinly pneumatic that a hard landing on a solid surface can shatter a wing. On this plate the skeleton is drawn schematically: fused carpometacarpus, reduced ulna, a knee that is really a tucked ankle, and a very, very long foot.

skeleton · 0.9 mm walls
03

Reading the same air

Cyclists hunt for slipstream; pelicans hunt for the sea-effect lift line a metre above the swell, and for thermals off sand. Both solve identical fluid-dynamics problems with near-identical energy cost per kilometre. The V-formation and the peloton are the same machine, separated by eighty million years.

aero · 52 km/h cruise
04

On the subject of the saddle

Sitting bone geometry is poor on a bird built for perching, so the load travels down the keel of the sternum and into the seat post. Note the forward pitch of the torso: the hips must reach the pedals, and the pedals must reach the crank, and the crank does not negotiate.
