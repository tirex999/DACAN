Field log · 07:12, low tide

He comes down the boardwalk at the hour when the light is thin and the sandpipers are already gone. Two things are always the same: the bell, and the pouch.

The bell first, three long rings from the dune end, so that everyone on the decks knows the fish delivery has arrived — he is delivering nothing, he is delivering himself, and he has never once paid for the coffee he buys with a mackerel. Then the pouch. It fills, empties, fills, on a cycle of about a second and a half, and the whole bird rocks with it, so that he appears to be a small boat under sail, going remarkably fast in a direction the water has no opinion about.

Watch the feet. There is no ankle to speak of, so the pedal is driven from the hip, and the whole webbed foot stays flat on the platform the way a swimmer keeps a paddle square — 380 square centimetres of it, the largest drivetrain surface of any cyclist on this coast and the only one that doubles as a serving tray.

At the corner where the deck boards go grey he stands on the left pedal, the front wheel lifting about a hand’s width — never more, there are gulls on the promenade and he has a reputation as a gull — and then he is away down the straight, bill level on the bar, wings folded like a coat he refuses to take off indoors.

He is not, on the evidence, trying to win anything. The low tide ends in eleven minutes, the sun is coming over the point, and the light is doing that thing it does in the half hour before it is worth photographing.

“Never once used the gears — there are none, and he considers the question vulgar.” Marginalia, pattern book, 1889
