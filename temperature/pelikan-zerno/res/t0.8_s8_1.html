§ 01

Field notes

The specimen arrived at the dune road at 11:42, pedalling in the manner of a creature who has read about bicycles and grasped the general principle. Forward motion was achieved. Dignity was not attempted. The scarf — red, absurd, entirely decorative — streamed aft at roughly the angle of a bird who has somewhere to be and no intention of hurrying.

Observe the seat disc: absent. A pelican on a saddle is a pelican balancing, which is to say a pelican using its throat as a fifth contact point. The gular pouch inflates and settles against the saddle horn, forming a living, slightly damp cushion. It is not comfortable. It is ingenious.

He does not steer with the wings. He steers with the pouch, which he has never been told is a steering apparatus.

Wing-braking was recorded twice, both times on the descent toward the tide line, both times with theatrical spread and a great deal of flapping for a speed of nine kilometres per hour. The fish in the handlebar basket was caught before departure and has therefore missed the entire journey. He rings the bell at dunes. He rings the bell at gulls. He rings the bell for the pleasure of hearing it.
