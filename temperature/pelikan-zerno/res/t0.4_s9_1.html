Section 02 — Annotations

Anatomy of a
pedalling waterbird

Seven features separate the velocipede pelican from its sedentary, fish-gulping cousins. Hover a number on the plate — or a line below — to pair them up.

  1. Gular pouch, Mk IV

    Inflated at speed it acts as a low windscreen: spray deflects downward, the resident sprat stays dry, and the bird gains a respectable 6% on the estuary straight.

    Aerodynamics

  2. Cranial shell

    A forty-gram polycarbonate dome, coral-lacquered. The plume escaping at the rear is unrepentantly decorative and adds measurable drag.

    Protection

  3. Wing–bar interface

    The humerus rests along a swept bar; the primary feathers curl beneath it and double as brake levers. Steering is done entirely with the tail.

    Control

  4. Caudal rudder

    Tail feathers fan to twenty-two degrees, stabilising the chassis in crosswinds and, on occasion, signalling an overtake to a slower heron.

    Stability

  5. Webbed drive flanges

    Full webbing across each foot turns a 48-tooth ring at 96 rpm. No cleats, no clips — the foot simply will not unstick itself.

    Propulsion

  6. Rear contact patch

    A 78-unit hoop at high pressure, tuned for boardwalk planking. The amber fender exists to keep the tail feathers out of the spokes.

    Rolling

  7. Cargo basket

    Three sprats, a flask of herring brine, and a folded map of the estuary — the last item unused, since the bird navigates by grudge.

    Logistics
