The specimen

A sentence built for every key.

In Japan, a shikakei is the sentence a repair shop types to prove a machine works — one pass, every key, no excuses.

The English-language specimen that stuck is gloriously absurd: a pelican, a bicycle, and a pouch full of fish. It stresses the shift keys, the wide reach of the Q, and the typist's patience with punctuation. Repairmen still tell you they can hear a stiff R from across the counter.

  1. The pouch does the lifting

    Drawn as a separate shape so it can wobble independently — 2.2s loop, anchored at the jawline.

  2. Legs are animated paths

    Each leg is a quadratic curve whose foot point traces a 38-unit crank circle in splined steps.

  3. Parallax in three planes

    Lamp posts, plank seams and dune grass scroll at 6s, 0.95s and 2.1s per repeat. Pointer motion nudges them.

  4. The bell is real audio

    Two detuned sines with a fast decay, built on first click. The clapper wobbles in SVG.
