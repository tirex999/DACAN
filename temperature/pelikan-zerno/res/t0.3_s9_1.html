Field notes / 02

The bird, the bike,
and the physics of audacity.

Four observations from the marsh road — three of them ornithological, one of them a lie we agreed to tell together.

01

The pouch is a net, not a pocket

The gular pouch is a distensible fishing basket stretched by a single elastic tendon. A brown pelican scoops fish at the surface, drains the water, then swallows head-first. It carries a catch, it does not store one — and it has never once held a packed lunch.

Capacity ≈ 11 litres · drag coefficient: considerable
02

Wings built for doing nothing

Up to 2.5 metres across. Soaring on a coastal thermal costs a pelican almost nothing — heart rate barely above resting. On a bicycle the same aerodynamic patience becomes a 2.5-metre front profile in a crosswind. Handlebars, accordingly, are wide.

Span 2.5 m · wingbeat while cruising: 0 / min
03

It falls on purpose

Brown pelicans plunge-dive from as much as 20 metres, folding back and hitting water at roughly 40 km/h. Compressible air sacs beneath the chest skin cushion the impact. A bird that survives that is not going to be frightened by a pothole.

Dive height 20 m · impact 40 km/h · helmet: a cap
04

The pedalling is apocryphal

No fossil, feather, or footage supports pelican cycling. Ankle joints bend the wrong way for a circular stroke, feet lack opposable anything, and the beak's moment arm on a drop bar is a structural joke. So we built the stroke in software: two-bone inverse kinematics, one crank, one very patient bird.

Legs solved live at 60 fps · hip → knee → foot

A full nictitating membrane, plus a brow line that reads, in field conditions, as pure contempt for traffic.

Purely decorative, physically expensive. Solved as a damped trailing chain so it whips with cadence.
