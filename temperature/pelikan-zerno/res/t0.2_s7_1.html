Le Pélicanà Bicyclette

The pelican on a bicycle — a study in balance

Sixteen hundred grams of bird, eleven kilos of steel, and a gular pouch full of audacity. Watch the near-side pedal: the webbed foot never leaves the block, even at ninety revolutions.

72 rpm

Fig. 2 — construction

Anatomy
of a feat

Hover a line. The drawing answers. Everything below is drawn, not photographed — two wheels, one bird, and a chain that has never once dropped.

  1. 01

    The mandible

    le bec · 41 cm

    Straight as a ruling pen, tipped with a hook he uses for nothing at all. He steers with it when the wind comes off the salt.

  2. 02

    The gular pouch

    la poche · 11 litres

    Fully distended it holds eleven litres of water and, on the day of this plate, one startled mullet. The pouch doubles as a saddlebag; he refuses to be separated from lunch.

  3. 03

    The primaries

    les rémiges · jet black

    Four black fingers wrapped around the grips. They carry 60% of the steering input and all of the personality.

  4. 04

    The saddle

    la selle · leather

    Set 12 mm low on purpose. A pelican cannot fully extend a tibio-tarsal joint, so the saddle takes up the difference — the secret of every pelican bicycle.

  5. 05

    Chainring, 52 teeth

    le plateau · 52 × 14

    Geared for a bird who weighs more than the bicycle. At 72 rpm the wheels turn 2.6 times per crank revolution; the chain has never dropped.

  6. 06

    The webbed foot

    le pied palmé · 14 cm

    Full webbing, no ankle to speak of. The foot presses the whole pedal, front to back, which is why the stroke looks liquid and never bobs.

Field notes — salt road, 18:40

“He did not fall.
That is the whole of
the criticism.”

— H. Laroque, Keeper of Plates, notebook 12

The observation lasted forty minutes. The bird mounted without assistance, waited for a heron to pass, and set off against a wind from the west. Cadence was steady, the pouch was balanced fore-and-aft, and the bell was used exactly twice — once at the bridge, once at the café, where he was served a sardine and change for the machine.

Note 01 · mounted

Mounting, unaided

He swung the near leg over while the machine was still rolling, which requires either courage or a very low saddle. Both, in fact.

Note 02 · the machine

A bicycle, mostly standard

Steel frame, coral lacquer, one bell, one basket. The basket contains a baguette, which the bird neither ate nor admitted to carrying.
