Drawing notes

How the loop is rigged

One requestAnimationFrame loop drives everything: crank angle, counter-rotating pedals (so the feet stay flat), wheel spin, parallax on palms, sea, road and grit, plus a two-bone IK solver for each leg. The scarf is resampled every frame from a travelling sine wave whose amplitude follows the throttle.

Field notes

Measured on Route 1, tide permitting. Numbers rounded, bird unbothered.

§ 03 / notes
30 mph

Cruising ceiling

Pelicans fly at 30 mph with a tailwind, which is roughly what this bike does at 1.8× throttle. They are, aerodynamically, overqualified.

11 L

Throat volume

The gular pouch out-holds the stomach — around three US gallons of water and fish. Honking costs about 0.4 L of it.

2.7×

Wheel ratio

Spins faster than the crank, slower than the gossip in the reed beds.

0

Hands on the bar

Wings only. Braking is by paddling backwards, which works beautifully in water and not at all on tarmac.

16 lb

Rider weight

Brown pelicans average sixteen pounds, hollow-boned, with a keel built for downstrokes. On the saddle that reads as a climbing cat with a appetite problem.

14

Wing feathers, per grip

Fanned across the handlebar for steering. The far wing is drawn 8 px lower and 12 % darker for depth.
