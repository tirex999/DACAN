field notes · boardwalk transect 4bobserved 18:41

The subject approached from the dune path at a steady, unembarrassed pace — no hands near the bars, neck locked, throat sac swinging like a water balloon at a fair. Wings stayed folded; a deliberate refusal to fly that we can only describe as a lifestyle choice.

At the boardwalk gate the pelican slowed, clipped both webbed feet out with the practiced indifference of a commuter who has done this eight hundred times, and allowed three photographs. Wind at the time was 4 m/s from the sea, favouring everyone except the gulls.

Notable: a single sprat aboard, alive, tail-deep in the front basket. The bell was not fitted as factory equipment.

plate 02 · anatomy of the machinehover a hotspot

Every part of this bird is a
aerodynamic apology.

It is built for falling into the sea head‑first, not for a 700c wheelbase — so everything has been negotiated. Six details, listed in the order the pelican thought of them.

  1. Gular pouch

    Net volume 11 L; drag coefficient, wet, in the region of a small sail. Used for: braking, cargo, one (1) sprat.

  2. Prehensile upper mandible

    Single tooth-equivalent. Steers with the beak at 22 km/h, which is the same trick as steering with your chin.

  3. Folded primaries

    2.30 m of stowed wing, kept tucked for commuter aerodynamics and unrolled only on the descent.

  4. Webbed pedal stroke

    Four‑point contact per pedal. 61 rpm on the flat; the webbing does most of the pulling up.

  5. Cargo basket & bell

    Woven reed, hand‑fitted after market. The bell is the only item on this bicycle the pelican did not catch.

  6. Wheel & fender

    Rim‑brake‑less. Stopping is performed by the pelican, who considers it understated.

HONK

Drawn in one file

Every limb on that pelican is solved per frame: the shins follow the pedals using the same two‑bar trigonometry a bike‑fit studio uses, the wheels and cranks are geared 2.35 : 1, and the whole scene is one speed dial. No images, no libraries.
