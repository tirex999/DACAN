Anatomy of
the maneuver

Four observations from 44 hours of roadside sketching, harbour-side wind, one very patient bird.

01
Aerodynamics

The pouch as a fairing

Streamlined at speed, the gular pouch lies flat against the lower mandible and reads as a hand-shaped front fairing. Loaded with fish, it flaps like a parachute — which is why Squadron 12 rides fasting.

02
Contact points

Two-point stability

The near wing rests on the bars; the webbed feet do the pulling. Between them the body stays upright through a full crank revolution, knees tracking forward in a clean, if ungainly, circle.

03
Braking

No thumbs, no brakes

There is no lever to grab. Deceleration is achieved by wind, by pride, and by the occasional dramatic wing-spread that stops spectators mid-sentence and, eventually, the bird.

04
Endurance

The 40-km coastal cruise

Filament feathers, hollow bones, a 2.4 gear ratio and an unreasonable fondness for headwinds. The bird finishes warm, dry, and loudly satisfied with itself.
