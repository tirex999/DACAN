Field notes

Three observations from the spit road, filed before the tide took the tracks.

01Morphology

The pouch is a net, not a saddlebag.

A brown pelican's gular pouch can hold roughly eleven litres — comfortably more than the bird's own stomach. It is deployed as a scoop: the bird folds its wings into a wedge, enters the water head-first, and lifts a mouthful of fish, weed and seawater. On the bicycle the pouch is used exclusively for dramatic flapping and aerodynamic complaint.

02Propulsion

Webbed feet make excellent, if illegal, cleats.

Plantars are strapped over the pedals; the knee acts as a pure piston, tracing a 44-millimetre circle at roughly ninety-three revolutions per minute. Leg strokes are driven forward by the hip, so the downstroke is long and the recovery short — the same shape a racing cyclist pays a fortune to buy.

03Provenance

The machine predates the bird's lineage of riders.

TheMichaux pedal-machine of 1867 is older than the last recorded great-great-grandpeliculation, which means the tradition is bird-follows-machine. Specimens appear on coast roads from here to the pier; all are white, all wear a scarf, none have ever been seen dismounted.
