Fig. 1 · Airframe & drivetrain

Ten parts that make the ride fly

Hover a line to light up the schematic. The bird was not engineered for bicycles; the bicycle was engineered for the bird.

  1. 01

    Gular strut & pouch

    Doubles as ballast tank, radiator and cargo net. One (1) baguette at a time, and the bicycle will not track straight.

  2. 02

    Control surfaces (wings)

    Fused to the handlebar. Left wing steers, right wing signals, both wings honk.

  3. 03

    Hip pivot

    92 mm femur + 92 mm shin, mounted 545 mm above the promenade. Reach envelope covers the whole 34 mm crank circle with slack.

  4. 04

    The true ankle

    What reads as a backward knee is an ankle. Two-bone IK pins the webbed foot to the pedal pin at all times — no clips, no shoes, no socks.

  5. 05

    Pedal pin

    34 mm radius, 6 mm platform, sanded for salt. Sole rests, toes grip.

  6. 06

    Crank & chainring

    42 teeth, single speed, sealed against brine. Chain is the only part that rusts; everything else is featherweight.

  7. 07

    Saddle / perch

    12 mm of drop under a 9 kg bird. Heel of the saddle, tail feathers braced against the seat stay.

  8. 08

    Tail vane

    Steering damper. Fuffs to 40° when braking, which is also the brake.

Field notes · three observations

Things seen on the promenade

06:52 · catch, secured

Zero fish, one fish

He can carry exactly one. Two is a philosophical position he has not yet been tested against. The basket is front-mounted so the tail can be watched approvingly.

06:58 · wind, off the water

Four knots, all of them rude

Crosswind at the breakwater. He leans into it like a commuter with somewhere to be, which he does — the bread gets cold.

07:04 · puncture, rear

The tyre, the oyster, the verdict

One thorn from the sea-grass, one oyster-catcher giving commentary. He sat on the kerb and inflates by mouth, dignified, pouch full of salt air.
